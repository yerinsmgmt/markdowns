# Orientation call: what to ask

Open this during the call. Add to it when a better question comes up.

---

## Open

> Thanks for making time. Before we plan anything I want to understand how your
> team actually works, so most of this is me asking and you talking.

---

## 1. Which team

- Which team are we doing this for?
- How many people are in it?
- Who does what in there?

---

## 2. How the work moves

- Walk me through how the work actually moves. Where does it start, where does
  it end?
- Who touches it along the way?
- How long does that take, start to finish?
- What is the part that slows everything down?

---

## 3. What they use now

- What tools are you using for that?
- Is any of it automated, or is it all done by hand?
- Where do you keep the information? Spreadsheets, a system, WhatsApp?
- Is there anything you have paid for that nobody really uses?

---

## 4. What they already know about AI

Two separate questions. **People say no to the first and yes to the second**,
and the second one is the real answer.

- Is anyone using AI for work here already? What for?
- And outside work? Do you use ChatGPT or anything like it for your own things?
- What did you use it for last?
- Was it any good?

---

## 5. For the owner

Ask these to whoever is paying, not to the room. **Say the outcome back to
them and let them agree to it.**

- If [that thing] took a day instead of a week, what would that change for you?
- Would you rather it was faster, or that fewer things went wrong?
- If this works on this team, which team would you want next?
- Is there anything happening this quarter that this needs to fit around?

---

## 6. Dates, before anybody leaves

- You have [n] session(s). Does [date] or [date] work, both at [time] your
  time?
- Anybody who cannot make either of those?

Type the date into the admin session page while they are still on the call, so
the invitation lands before they hang up.

---

## What to suggest, by team

Only after they have told you what they do. **Suggest, do not open with it.**

**Customer support.** Drafting replies to the questions that come in fifty
times a day, writing the answer once and reusing it, summarising a long
complaint before replying, turning a call into notes.

**Sales and field agents.** Writing up visits and reports without typing them
out, turning notes into a follow-up message, preparing for a meeting from
whatever is already on file.

**Finance and collections.** Chasing payments in the customer's own words,
turning a spreadsheet into a plain summary, checking numbers against each
other, first draft of a report.

**Operations and logistics.** Turning a mess of updates into one daily
picture, writing procedures somebody will actually read, first pass at a
schedule or a rota.

**Marketing.** Rewriting one piece of copy for four channels, first drafts in
a brand voice, turning a product change into an announcement.

**HR and people.** Job adverts, first sift of applications, writing policies in
plain language, turning an appraisal conversation into notes.

**Leadership.** Turning ten reports into one page, drafting the difficult
email, preparing for a board meeting from the documents.

---

## Write down afterwards, same day

- Who was on the call, and what each said they do
- How the work moves, in their words
- The tools they named
- How long it takes now
- What the owner said they wanted
- The dates agreed

**The "how long it takes now" is the one that matters.** Without it there is
nothing to compare against afterwards, and no case study.
